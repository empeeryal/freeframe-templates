Brineworks: an oxygen alarm 140 catfish farms trust at 2 a.m.

Two founders in Stuttgart, Arkansas, had a pond sensor that worked and an app that farmers ignored. In 14 weeks we rebuilt the app around one moment, the night alarm, and launched it through the feed stores farmers already trust.

01 / The problem

The sensor was right. Nobody looked at the app in time.

Catfish ponds lose oxygen fastest between midnight and dawn. When it drops below about 3 mg/L, a farm can lose a pond of fish in an hour. Brineworks’ sensor caught the drop. Their first app showed it on page three of a dashboard built for daytime, on a laptop.

Farm managers told us the same thing in different words: at 2 a.m., in a truck, with gloves on, they need one number, one color and one button. Everything else could wait for breakfast.

“If I have to log in, the fish are already dead.” — a farm manager, week 1

02 / From sketch to shipped

Week 3 on paper. Week 12 in the store.

Drag the handle to compare the sketch we tested with six farm managers against the screen that shipped. The layout barely moved; the type got bigger and the button got louder.

  • 01One number per pond, readable at arm’s length in a dark cab.
  • 02The aerator button works from the lock screen alert.
  • 03Night colors by default from sunset, so the screen never blinds.

03 / What shipped

Three things, all live on launch day.

The app

iOS and Android, React Native. Night mode from sunset, alarms that break through Do Not Disturb, and a farm map for the morning.

The alert service

Takes a reading from every sensor each minute, spots a falling trend 25 minutes early and pages the right person, then the next one.

The dealer kit

A counter card, a setup video and a 20-minute training so feed store staff could install sensors and sign farms up on the spot.
