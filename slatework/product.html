Six steps, one system, no hand-offs lost.

Each part of Slatework passes its work to the next: a posting fills the pipeline, the pipeline books interviews, scorecards decide offers and offers become hires. Try each part below.

01 Sourcing and job boards

Write the posting once. Send it everywhere it should go.

Pick boards, set the pay range and see the posting as candidates will. Free boards cost nothing; paid boards are billed through Slatework, so finance gets one invoice.

    02 Shared pipeline

    Stages that do the follow-up for you.

    Add rules to any stage: send the scheduling link when someone enters Interview, ask for scorecards when the interview ends, and flag anyone who has waited too long.

    Try the live pipeline board on the home page

    1. 1

      When a candidate enters Screen

      Send the phone screen booking link and assign the recruiter on the req.

    2. 2

      When the interview ends

      Ask each interviewer for their scorecard, with a reminder in chat after 20 hours.

    3. 3

      When someone waits 5 days in any stage

      Flag the card amber and add it to the weekly hiring review.

    4. 4

      When a candidate is rejected

      Send a kind, specific note within 48 hours, using the template for their stage.

    03 Structured interviews

    A scorecard that does the math.

    Weight each competency by how much it matters for the role. Interviewers rate 1 to 4, and the weighted score turns into a recommendation before the debrief starts.

      04 Scheduling

      Panels across four time zones, booked in one step.

      Slatework reads each interviewer’s working hours and calendar, then proposes times that work for everyone. Candidates see them in their own time zone; interviewers see their own.

      Interview panel

        Working hours 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. in each interviewer’s own time, on weekdays.

          05 Offers and approvals

          Offers checked against the band before anyone sees them.

          Slatework compares every offer with the pay band for the role and routes it to the right approvers. Inside the band, the hiring manager signs off. Outside it, the people who own the budget do.
